Your posting describes the website as the front door for most residents. That
is how I worked at the Harbourline Archive: the collection only reached people
when the page that led to it was clear.

For five years I was the archive's digital editor. I wrote and edited around
120 collection stories and ran the website in Drupal. I also designed the
content model that links each story to the archive records behind it. The
"Maps of the Harbour" series became the most-read part of the site. It was
written for visitors, not for archivists.

Your mix of practical information and local history is familiar ground. At the
archive I rewrote the visitor pages in plain language and tested them with
visitors. Questions about opening hours dropped after that. Twelve branches is
more than I have kept current at once. I would start with a shared structure
for the branch pages, so updates take minutes instead of a search.

Writing editorial guidelines for contributors is also not new to me: six
colleagues wrote for the archive site with the guidelines I set up.

I'd welcome the chance to explain my motivation in a conversation. My CV is
attached.
